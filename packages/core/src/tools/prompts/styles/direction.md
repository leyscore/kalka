# Colour and type direction

When the person gives colours or fonts, use them. Otherwise choose them in this order.

1. **The subject's own world first.** Before any colour, name four concrete things the subject is made of or lives among: materials, places, tools, signage, the era (a Nantes bistro: zinc counter, slate board, enamel jugs, bottle green shutters). Take the ground, the ink and the accent from those things, and say which thing each colour comes from. Take the display face from the letters that already exist in that world (a shop sign, a stencil, a menu board, a label, a timetable).
2. **The domain table second.** If the world gives you nothing precise, find the subject's domain below and take the option that fits it best, not the first one you reach for. Adjust its values to the subject; do not mix options.
3. **Never your recent defaults.** These are the drafts you produce without thinking, so they need a reason from step 1 to be used: a pale cool grey ground (around #F3F5F4), a vermilion or red accent, Hanken Grotesk, Bricolage Grotesque, Familjen Grotesk, Public Sans.

Screens of an app or a dashboard use the same palette with a quieter hand: the ground and surfaces come from its neutrals, the accent is kept for the one primary action and the current selection, and states (late, paid, error) get their own hues that are not the accent.
