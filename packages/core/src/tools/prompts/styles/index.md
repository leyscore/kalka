# Style index

Ten styles. Read each entry and apply the selection rules at the bottom. Load the chosen style's full guide with `read_style`, named in lowercase with hyphens (`swiss-grid`, `de-stijl`); never load a guide you did not choose. Choosing no style is allowed.

## Editorial

- **Looks like:** a well-set magazine or long-form site. Serif body at 20 px on warm paper, a narrow text column with notes in the margin, rules that open sections.
- **Good for:** publications, essays, think tanks, authors, galleries, wine and food writing, law and consulting firms that sell judgement, annual reports, restaurant menus read as text.
- **Avoid for:** dev tools, dashboards, consumer apps, anything whose main job is a fast task.
- **Photos:** helps but not needed. Fallback: a type-only opener with a drop cap, a pull quote at 36 px, a captioned placeholder at a real crop ratio below the fold.
- **Signature:** text column with a live margin of notes; masthead; headline-dek-byline stack.

## Swiss Grid

- **Looks like:** flush-left grotesque on white, strong scale jumps, everything hung from a 12-column grid you feel through alignment rather than see.
- **Good for:** transit and wayfinding, typography and type foundries, rationalist architecture firms, data publications and statistics offices, design studios.
- **Avoid for:** anything warm, consumer finance, food, wellness, children, and any subject that is not itself systematic.
- **Photos:** optional. Fallback: type as image at 128–160 px, one flat colour field, a timetable or data table as the visual.
- **Signature:** a poster-scale word; hanging labels in columns 1–3; one red or ultramarine field.

## Warm Organic

- **Looks like:** paper-toned grounds, a soft serif display at light weight, food or craft colour, generous spacing, a hand-made accent used once.
- **Good for:** bakeries, restaurants, coffee, farms, ceramics, florists, wellness, local shops, food brands, children's products.
- **Avoid for:** enterprise software, any money product (banks, insurance, payments, savings, even for young or friendly audiences), legal, dev tools, anything that must read as precise.
- **Photos:** wants them. Fallback: a big ingredient word at 160 px on a tinted field, a menu board hero, alternating paper tints with captions.
- **Signature:** menu set like a list with dotted leaders; one round stamp; a full-bleed colour band.

## Dark Precision

- **Looks like:** near-black or warm-dark ground, crisp grotesque at tight tracking, product UI drawn true size as the hero, one accent.
- **Good for:** developer tools, AI and agent products, infrastructure, terminals, editors, API platforms, observability.
- **Avoid for:** consumer banking, health, food, children, local businesses.
- **Photos:** no. Fallback is the product: an app window, a terminal, a command palette, code tabs.
- **Signature:** product frame cropped by the fold with a floating menu over its edge; white primary button; 6–8% hairlines.

## Corporate Trust

- **Looks like:** white ground, one saturated brand colour, plain sans at 18–19 px body, softly rounded controls (6–8 px radius), sourced numbers, forms and tables done the service-design way.
- **Good for:** banks, payments, insurance, pensions, health providers, clinics, public services, payroll and tax tools. This includes friendly, young or consumer money products: make the style warmer (a brighter brand colour, rounder shapes, plainer words) rather than leave it.
- **Avoid for:** culture, fashion, food, studios, anything that should feel expressive.
- **Photos:** optional. Fallback: a phone with a real task screen, a calculator hero, a fee table.
- **Signature:** a task-first hero; numbers with a source line; a fee table with 48 px rows.

## Bento

- **Looks like:** one section of unequal rounded tiles on a 12-column grid, one lead tile at least twice the area of any other, visuals bleeding off the tile edges.
- **Good for:** product feature overviews, hardware, consumer apps with several capabilities of similar weight, launch pages, release notes.
- **Avoid for:** long reading, services sold on trust, anything with one main message. Never as the whole page.
- **Photos:** no. Fallback: UI fragments, one large number, a small chart, a key cap, a device crop.
- **Signature:** lead tile first in the markup; 4–8 tiles in at most 3 sizes; nested radius smaller than the tile radius.

## Blueprint

- **Looks like:** a technical drawing: light grey or cyanotype-blue ground with white lines, mono labels, thin dimension lines, a title block, hatching, revision tables.
- **Good for:** hardware, robotics, aerospace, manufacturing, engineering firms, architecture practices that draw, specs, developer hardware kits, audio gear.
- **Avoid for:** consumer lifestyle, food, wellness, finance for individuals.
- **Photos:** no. Fallback: a line drawing in SVG with callouts, an exploded view, an X-in-a-box figure with a FIG caption.
- **Signature:** title block in the corner; dimension lines with ticks; a revision table whose letters skip I and O.

## Cinematic

- **Looks like:** a film title sequence: huge condensed or high-contrast type, dark or deep-colour frames, credits in tracked caps, scenes 160–240 px apart.
- **Good for:** films, studios, music, theatre, festivals, hotels, fashion, galleries, photographers, events.
- **Avoid for:** dashboards, utilities, anything with a form at its centre, services sold on detail.
- **Photos:** helps but must look strong without. Fallback, in this order: title card with billing block, colour-field frames, index list, contact sheet of captioned frames.
- **Signature:** title card at 160–240 px; billing block in condensed caps at no less than 15% of the title size; full-height frames at a projection ratio with a 5% safe area.

## Bauhaus

- **Looks like:** a 1920s school print job on warm paper: lowercase geometric sans or heavy wide grotesque at masthead size, heavy black bars, photos set into the text, red and black with one primary as a large field, at most three grid-snapped shapes, asymmetric layout.
- **Good for:** design and art schools, studios, architecture practices, museums and cultural programmes, workshops and maker spaces, exhibition sites.
- **Avoid for:** any money product (banks, insurance, payments), health and clinics, legal, food and hospitality, children's consumer brands, dev tools and dashboards sold as products, anything that must feel soft or luxurious.
- **Photos:** optional. Fallback: a lowercase masthead over a black bar, nine squares after Schmidt's exercises, construction drawings of the name, colour fields opening each section.
- **Signature:** lowercase display at 112–200 px; one 18 px black bar; programme set as a real table.

## De Stijl

- **Looks like:** a Mondrian or Rietveld composition as a page: mostly white planes divided by black bars on the grid gutters, a few unequal planes of red, yellow and blue, only horizontals and verticals.
- **Good for:** art galleries and museums, furniture makers and showrooms, architecture and interior studios, design shops, exhibition microsites.
- **Avoid for:** banks and money products, health, food and hospitality, children's brands, dev tools and data-dense SaaS marketing, anything warm or organic; never a quilt of equal coloured blocks or a fake Mondrian as hero art.
- **Photos:** optional (objects and rooms shot square-on). Fallback: a composition of planes and bars carrying the title, the site's sections as a plan of unequal planes, a specification table set in its plane.
- **Signature:** 24 px black bars in the gutters, at least one running to the viewport edge; unequal primary planes under 12% of the area; radius 0.

## Selection rules

1. **Choose by subject fit, not by safety.** Ask which style the subject's own world already speaks. A bakery speaks Warm Organic, an observability tool speaks Dark Precision, a robotics firm speaks Blueprint, a design school speaks Bauhaus, a modular furniture maker speaks De Stijl.
2. **Swiss Grid is not a default.** Use it only when the subject itself is systematic: transit, typography, rationalist architecture, data publications, design studios. In the last run it was picked 6 times out of 10 and the pages came out ruled everywhere. If you are choosing it because it feels safe, choose again.
3. **"Avoid for" rules a style out.** If the subject is on a style's avoid list, that style is off the table, whatever the audience or tone; soften a fitting style instead (a friendlier palette, rounder shapes). A teen bank or a renters' insurance is still a money product.
4. **When two styles fit, take the less common one** in this order of commonness, most common first: Dark Precision, Swiss Grid, Corporate Trust, Bento, Editorial, Warm Organic, Cinematic, Blueprint, Bauhaus, De Stijl.
5. **Bento is a section, not a page.** You may use it once inside another style. Record both: `style: Dark Precision + Bento section`.
6. **Mixing.** Take the grid from one style and colour and type from at most one other. One accent, two type families.
7. **No style is an answer.** When no entry's "Good for" speaks the subject, or the person's brand already sets the look, use none: build the direction from the subject's world with the global rules and the colour and type direction. Never bend a subject to fit a style.
8. **With a person present**, offer the choice during the kickoff (ask_user when it exists): the 2 or 3 styles that fit, each with a one-line reason and a palette, plus "none of these, my own direction" and "I'll send a reference image". When find_references exists, search it for the fitting styles applied to the subject and pass the results as the images of that question, so the person sees them rather than reads names. Let them pick one, combine two by rule 6, or refuse all. Their answer wins over these rules.
9. **Always record the choice**, with or without a person: write the HTML comment on the first line of the page, `<!-- style: … | reason: … | palette: ground / ink / accent -->`. Kalka keeps it on the page, and read_style without a name reads it back in a later session.
